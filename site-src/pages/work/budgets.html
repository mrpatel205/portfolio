Everyone budgets their finances in one way or another. The bank's September 2023 Financial Wellbeing Research ranked budgeting as a priority for FY24, particularly among customers aged 18-35 with low-to-medium financial control or resilience.

That cohort and others, however, had no way to track spending against a budget in-app, research showed they used spreadsheets or self-made "bucket" accounts instead. The bank also lagged behind competing banking apps such as CBA, which offered its own budgeting tool linked to customers' spending data.

I was the sole designer and researcher on Budgets from MVP through development handover; the prior team's experience canvas set the initial problem framing. I owned research, design, concept testing, and interaction design end-to-end, working directly with the product owner, business analyst, and engineering stakeholders.

The problem was already framed when I joined; how to solve it wasn't.

I ran two rounds of research and testing to define and refine the approach to the budgets feature and worked close with my BA and the accessibility team to produce the final detailed figma artefacts to hand over to engineering.

Budgets was scoped against Accounts, backed by one PM; the other PM, the BA, the principal designer and I disagreed, convinced customers reasoned in categories. Ten more concepts from ideation sat unranked, with FY26 roadmap priorities resting on them. One test had to settle the split and order the roadmap, using a sample too thin to trust.

With the concept test too thin to call outright, the deciding factor turned technical: categories carried no dependency on a separate uplift workstream, unlike accounts, cutting delivery risk. That combination, inconclusive desirability data plus lower risk, was enough to align both PMs behind the pivot to categories.

Budgeting matters most to customers with the least room to spare, people tracking every dollar week to week. I engaged financial hardship counsellors directly to understand their needs, which shaped the feature's content writing and built-in educational guidance, rather than treating inclusivity as a late-stage accessibility pass.

Budgets shared a mini-app with Spending because of a platform split between the existing iOS app and the new React Native build, which meant it inherited Spending's fixed per-category colours rather than getting its own. Ten of twelve customers testing category budgets couldn't tell at a glance which ones were over their limit.

Testing validated the full status-colour scheme, green for met, red for over. But the Spending mini app ran on a design system that had been frozen for further investment as the business moved other mini-apps to its replacement, and category colours themselves were already scoped for a separate, wider redefinition owned by another team.

Recolouring every category wasn't mine to ship.

Colour alone still asked customers to scan every row to find the one that mattered. So I extended the same logic to order: categories now auto-sort by how far over budget they are, worst first, so the one needing attention sits at the top without being searched for.

Adoption has been modest so far: around 4,000 customers logged in during August 2025, averaging 1.7 sessions a month. Across the app overall, those customers average 5.88 sessions, suggesting they're moving back and forth between Budgets and the Overview screen rather than using Budgets in isolation, checking in on other parts of their finances before returning to it.
